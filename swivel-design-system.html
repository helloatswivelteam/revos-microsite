1 · Typography scale

Museo Sans, loaded as four separate families

Critical. Museo Sans is loaded as four font families — Museo Sans 300, 500, 700, 900 — not four weights of one family. A CSS font-weight:600 on Museo Sans 700 is the 700 face. Always specify the family name, never the weight.

2b · Gradients

One brand gradient, fifteen angles

The single biggest inconsistency. #82c88a → #0196cd is rendered at ~15 different angles, with two stop patterns and a 3-stop variant. Same two colours, no canonical angle.
The brand gradient, as actually rendered
Soft tint section washes — the rhythm device

Full-span. Brand colours at 15–20% alpha bleed in at the top and/or bottom edge; the middle stays pure white. This is how the site separates sections without rules or solid blocks.

Radial accent glows & conic orb fills
Hard-stop split backgrounds

Used to sit a card half on a dark bed, half on a light one. Mobile flattens the split to a single charcoal.

Focus states

9 · Motion & scroll

All JS-driven; almost nothing is in CSS

MechanismDetail
Entrance (“Appear”)5 elements per page carry data-framer-appear-id; 79 instances site-wide. They render at opacity:0.001 with will-change:transform until triggered, then fade/translate to opacity:1.
Scroll-linked fadesOther sections (e.g. the homepage FAQ) fade in progressively as they enter the viewport — opacity tracks scroll position rather than firing once.
Transitionstransition:all on ~32,700 elements (Framer default). Real durations/easings are framer-motion in JS and not readable from CSS. The only explicit CSS transition is opacity 0.2s on carousel controls.
KeyframesExactly one exists site-wide: __framer-loading-spin (Framer's internal loader). No custom keyframe animation.
StaggerMulti-item grids show no measurable per-item stagger in the DOM; items share one appear group.
VideoThe homepage carries a full-bleed autoplaying background video section (Video Background, 781px) directly beneath the hero.

10 · Forms & inputs

The system has no form pattern

Across 75 pages, native <input> elements exist on exactly two. Every real conversion form — /audit, /contact, /newsletter, /schedule-meeting-calendar, /gtm-audit-start, /gtm-audit — is a third-party embed (HubSpot / scheduler). Its fields, validation and error states are not controlled by this design system.
PageFieldsStyling
/loginemail, password40px · bg rgba(10,15,30,.5) · border 1px rgba(255,255,255,.16) · radius 12px · pad 12/14 · 12px · caret #82c88a · label 13/15.6 MS700 #fff · submit #82c88a r12 h38
/pathforward-dashboardpassword38px · bg #fff · border 1px #e4e8ec · radius 8px · pad 10/12 · 14px · submit #0d9488 r8 h36

Both are React code components (PasswordGateLogin.tsx, Client_Dashboard.tsx), not Framer forms — which is why they use ui-sans-serif, #0d9488 and #e4e8ec, none of which exist in the design system.

Consequence. There is no design-system answer to “what does a text input look like?”, “what does a validation error look like?”, or “what does a multi-step form look like?” If a new page needs a native form, that pattern must be designed — it does not exist.

12 · Content flow

The narrative arc

credibility→ problem (named as their pain)→ system (named as Swivel's)→ proof (named clients, real numbers)→ objections→ one next action

Proof is deployed twice — once early for credibility, once late to close objections. Objections are handled explicitly and by name (“Does this actually work?”, “We know what you're thinking”, “The major drawbacks of BDRs”). The site argues rather than asserts.

The rules a never-before-seen block must follow to feel native. This is the section to use when designing new work.
